# Pricing Guide

Internal reference — what to quote and why. Adjust to your market, but
change the *numbers*, not the *structure*: one-time build + recurring care
plan is the model, because the care plan is what compounds.

## Tiers

| Tier | Build | Care plan | Who gets quoted this |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | $750 | $39/mo | Single-location service business, one-pager, template-driven (what `site build` produces) |
| **Standard** | $1,500 | $59/mo | Multi-page (services split out, gallery, testimonials), light custom design |
| **Premium** | $3,000+ | $99/mo | Booking/ordering integration, copywriting, photography coordination, rush delivery |

Quote based on the lead's revenue signals (score, review volume, category —
a roofing company books $10k jobs; quote Standard+ with confidence). When
unsure, quote one tier up and let them negotiate down to the tier you
expected — never the reverse.

## Rules that protect the business

- **50% deposit, no exceptions.** It's in the services agreement; the build
  doesn't start without it. People who won't pay a deposit won't pay an
  invoice.
- **Care plan is opt-out, not opt-in.** Present every proposal with the care
  plan included; let them remove it. ~$50/mo × 20 clients is $12k/yr of
  recurring revenue that costs a few hours a month.
- **Third revision round is a change order.** Quote it at $75–125/hr. The
  agreement backs you up — point to it kindly.
- **Annual price floor:** never let a care-plan client pay less than your
  hosting + time costs. Grandfathering old prices is fine; losing money on
  maintenance is not.

## The proposal

Use `templates/outreach/proposal.txt` — fill in the tier numbers, domain
idea, and timeline. Send it the same day as the "interested" call while
you're still fresh in their mind, with the agreement ready to go the moment
they say yes.
